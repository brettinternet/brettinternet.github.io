Apple's overpriced offering is moving away from modular, upgradable hardware.
Some recent unveilings of the iMac, Mac Pro, and MacBook Pro failed to meet the
expectations of many professionals who demand improved specifications. A
Hackintosh can provide an ideal developer environment by pairing Unix with more
powerful hardware. For example, at the time of writing, my build uses Kaby Lake,
while current Apple hardware doesn't offer the newest Intel generation. Kaby
Lake support is still experimental in the community, but more stable Skylake
options exist.

I first started developing on a MacBook. There are aspects of Apple's laptops
that other hardware manufacturers have not been able to match for me quite yet.
But for a desktop where form factor, sleek aluminum design, and the responsive
trackpad are not concerns, a Hackintosh is the way to go for tabletop computing.

Should you build a Hackintosh? Probably not. It's a breach of Apple's macOS
Terms and Conditions, and they
[have sued companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psystar_Corporation) that
attempt to turn a profit. However, Apple has been kind and
[hasn't litigated individuals](https://www.reddit.com/r/hackintosh/comments/2ek35g/is_it_technically_illegal/).
Building a Hackintosh involves a _lot_ of troubleshooting and tinkering. If
you're okay with that, start by lurking in the community
([/r/hackintosh](https://reddit.com/r/hackintosh)).

I've posted a
[guide to my most recent build](https://github.com/brettinternet/hackintosh/blob/8788216554bf169d0f7eff2581ab372e14ce3b2b/docs/setup.md).
The build uses the latest Kaby Lake Intel processor and motherboard, along with
an NVMe SSD, Bluetooth accessories, iMessage, and other necessary features. My
passion for Hackintosh fluctuates with my availability, but you can see my
builds, guides, and troubleshooting research at
[brettinternet/hackintosh](https://github.com/brettinternet/hackintosh).
