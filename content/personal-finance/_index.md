I grew up in a place where finances weren't openly discussed. I never learned in
high school or college the practical advice on money, but I was always careful
and had to work hard to afford what I own.

## What I've learned

My experience is constrained to being in the middle of my career in Middle
America while having dependents. I've interrogated a few financial advisors to
acquire the general template of what to do with money when you have it.

### Bills

Pay bills.
[Gordon B. Hinckley](https://www.churchofjesuschrist.org/study/ensign/2002/07/climbing-out-of-debt?lang=eng)
said:

> Pay off debt as quickly as you can, and free yourselves from bondage.

For most people, debt is more expensive than savings.

### Cash

Financial advisors all suggest building up an emergency fund of 6 months of
spending.

Keep this kind of money somewhere low-risk, such as a money market fund or
high-yield savings accounts. Cash has to be ready to move.

### Investments

Investments don't have to be as ready to move.

#### HSA

If you have a High-Deductible Health Plan, a Health Savings Account is the best
tax advantage for our class: tax-free contributions, tax-free growth, and
tax-free withdrawals for qualified medical expenses.

Average retired individuals in USA spend almost $200k on out-of-pocket medical
expenses, so this is also a form of retirement savings.

You can also save qualified medical receipts and refund yourself at any time,
even years later when you need it.

For the years we "acquired" our dependents, we switched back to a traditional
PPO.

#### Retirement

##### 401k

If your employer matches contributions, always contribute that amount to get the
benefit. Then max it out when you're able.

##### IRA

You and your spouse can contribute a certain amount each year.

#### 529

Calculate how much you want to set aside for high education, and work towards
that goal.

### Tips

- If you can control your spending, use only credit cards
    - Accumulate points or cash back
    - Avoid carrying a debit card which offer less security measures and
      benefits
- Freeze your credit with as many credit bureaus as you can
- Don't buy things you can't afford
